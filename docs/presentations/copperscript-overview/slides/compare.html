Simplified: each tool has scripting, plug-ins and exceptions. KiCad and Altium remain the reference for interactive, expert layout.

A fair comparison from the point of view of an AI agent. Graphical tools such as KiCad and Altium are mature and excellent for an expert who places and routes by hand; an agent, though, has to drive a GUI or edit drawing files whose meaning is geometry, and most of the engineering intent lives outside the design. Netlist-as-code tools such as SKiDL give the agent text to write, but mostly for connectivity, and the layout still happens in a GUI. CopperScript puts connectivity and intent in one typed source, answers with machine-readable findings, and runs layout as a bounded pipeline that reports why it failed. Its router is experimental; the GUI tools remain better for interactive layout, and CopperScript exports to KiCad so the two can be combined.
